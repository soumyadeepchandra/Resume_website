Soumyadeep Chandra

Graduate Research Assistant at Purdue University.

I am a Ph.D. researcher in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Purdue University, advised by Prof. Kaushik Roy at the Nanoelectronics Research Laboratory. My work connects AI and hardware through fast, physically grounded models.

  • Thermal modeling for 3D integrated circuits — Neural operators for steady-state and transient temperature prediction across stacked dies.
  • Physics-informed machine learning — Learning from FEM simulation while respecting heat flow, boundaries, and energy conservation.
  • Video understanding — Learning procedural structure and temporal alignment from video demonstrations.

I build simulation and data-generation pipelines alongside the models, so the methods can be tested on varied power maps and realistic workloads.

Research

Therm3D. A physics-aware neural operator for transient thermal analysis of 3D heterogeneous integration. It combines an automated COMSOL–Python data pipeline with coordinate-conditioned prediction and energy and boundary constraints. On unseen transient power maps, it reached 1.09 °C RMSE and approximately 10,000–20,000× faster inference than FEM.

2D-ThermAl. A physics-informed framework for circuit thermal analysis. It achieved 0.71 °C RMSE with approximately 200× faster inference than COMSOL FEM. Code →

OPEL and REMAP. Procedure learning and video alignment using optimal transport, including partially matched or unaligned demonstrations.
